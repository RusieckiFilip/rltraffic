## §"Metric"

### 1. The definition pair ρ is computed under — and the measurement that decides it

**Proposal: ρ is computed on the POOL-CLOCK, ALL-CREATED pair on both backends** — `att_engine` on
CityFlow (already primary on hz1x1 under Rule R) and **`E_sumo`** on SUMO — with the
ADMITTED-population pair (`att_ours`; the SUMO env's `average_travel_time`) **reported beside it in
every table**, extending A11(b)'s unconditional-disclosure discipline to SUMO.

**The reason is a measurement, not an argument about fairness.** `PREREGISTRATION` §3.4 normalises
*within* each backend precisely so that raw travel times never cross the boundary. That removes the
incomparability of LEVELS. It does not remove an incomparability of DEFINITIONS, and on 2026-08-31
the coordinator registered the threat that CityFlow has two ATT definitions and SUMO one, so
`ρ_cityflow` is definition-dependent while `ρ_sumo` is not. **Half A built SUMO's missing twin and
measured the consequence** (means of 5 episodes per arm; `fixedtime = 0`, `maxpressure = 1`):

| definition | Δ CityFlow | Δ SUMO | ratio | ρ_random CityFlow | ρ_random SUMO | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **admitted** (`att_ours`, SUMO env) | 37.6645 | 11.6050 | **3.246×** | −3.6154 | −9.6728 | **6.0574** |
| **pool-clock** (`att_engine`, `E_sumo`) | 235.1994 | 237.6252 | **1.010×** | −1.4750 | −1.1140 | **0.3610** |

⭐ **Under the admitted definitions the two backends' anchor spreads differ by 3.2× and ρ_random by
6.06. Under the pool-clock pair the spreads agree to within 1 % and ρ_random to within 0.36.** The
admitted pair is not measuring the same quantity on the two backends: CityFlow's admitted metric
excludes vehicles the insertion buffer never released, SUMO's excludes a *different* set on a
*different* clock, and the residue lands in the denominator of ρ. The pool-clock pair asks both
engines the same question — *how long did every vehicle the demand created spend waiting or
travelling, counted from when the demand created it* — and the two answers agree.

⚠️ **What this does NOT say.** It does not say the two engines behave alike; Δ agreeing to 1 % is a
statement about the ANCHOR SPREAD, not about the levels, which differ by 190–240 s. It does not
re-open P7.0's branch (§4). And it is one scenario, one intersection, nominal demand, 5 episodes per
arm.

### 2. The frozen SUMO regime: teleporting disabled

**Proposal: every later SUMO measurement runs the `noteleport` configuration** —
`scenarios/hangzhou_1x1_bc-tyc_18041610_1h_parity/hangzhou_1x1_bc-tyc_18041610_1h_parity_noteleport.sumocfg`,
a NEW file identical to the parity one plus `<processing><time-to-teleport value="-1"/></processing>`,
referencing the same shipped network and the same parity route file.

SUMO's `--time-to-teleport` defaults to **300 s** and neither the shipped nor the P7.0 parity
`.sumocfg` sets it; CityFlow never teleports. **So P7.0's SUMO cells, and half A's A1, ran with
teleporting ENABLED and must be labelled so wherever they are reported.**

Measured effect (means of 5, A1 against A1b):

| arm | env ATT | E_sumo | teleports/episode (A1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| maxpressure, teleports enabled | 360.0205 | 452.2003 | 13, 15, 14, 14, 13 |
| maxpressure, teleports disabled | 364.1588 | 461.4630 | 0, 0, 0, 0, 0 |
| **difference** | **+4.1383** | **+9.2627** | — |
| fixedtime | **bit-identical** between regimes | | 0 everywhere |
| random | **bit-identical** between regimes | | 0 everywhere |

Only the MaxPressure arm teleports at all, and disabling it makes that arm slightly *worse*, which is
the expected direction: a teleport removes a stuck vehicle and stops charging for it. The two
untouched arms being bit-identical is the control that the new file changes nothing else.

⚠️ Disabling teleports moves ρ: Δ_SUMO under the admitted pair falls 11.6050 → 7.4667 and ρ_random
goes −9.6728 → −15.0338, while under the pool-clock pair Δ moves 237.63 → 228.36 and ρ_random
−1.1140 → −1.1592. **The pool-clock reading is the stable one under the regime change as well.**

**The dominant term on both backends is CLOCK ORIGIN — the insertion-buffer wait of vehicles that DID
enter — not the never-admitted population.** That is P5.3b-fix's finding (94 % clock origin on
CityFlow `mix50`) recurring on a second backend and on all three anchors.

🔒 **SUMO's cadence term is EXACTLY `0.0` on all 30 observed SUMO episodes**, and the two counters
say why rather than leaving it to luck: `n_arrived_never_observed_at_a_boundary = 0` on every SUMO
row (no vehicle both departed and arrived inside one 10 s window, so the env metric's decision-grid
bookkeeping missed nobody) and `n_vanished_without_arrival = 0` everywhere. **On CityFlow it is
non-zero on all 15 rows (3.6743 … 4.7827)** — the window-midpoint estimator of `metrics/cityflow.py`.
⚠️ `BRIEF_34` §2.1 asserted the SUMO term was zero *by construction*; it is not — `metrics/sumo.py`
fills its population bookkeeping at the 10 s `update()` cadence and a short trip could evade it. It is
zero **as measured, on this scenario, at this demand**, and the counter that would detect otherwise is
reported per episode. A non-zero count on another scenario is a finding about the frozen
`metrics/sumo.py`, and an open question rather than a fix (Amendment A5).

The decomposition residual is reported and is **not** a gate: `(P−E)+(W−P)+(att−W)−(att−E)` cancels
identically, so it is an algebraic tautology (max observed `0.0`). The gating check is the clock
origin by its **second route** — `W−P` against the mean `getDepartDelay`, max observed **5.68e−14**
against a 1e−9 tolerance.

a property of the two engines' dynamics.** Under the teleport-free regime the same conclusion holds
(gap 0.315823 ≤ 0.737508).

### 5. SUMO's determinism, and the timing table with its `n`

**Determinism.** All **45/45** episodes reproduced their P7.0 cell, on both backends, under
`np.float32(fresh) == np.float32(stored)` — the exact form the corpus permits, because `att_per_step`
is stored `float32` while the env produces `float64`. Largest float64 gap **1.473e−05**; the check's
**detection floor is one float32 ulp** (~3e−5 at ATT 355), so drift below that is invisible to it by
construction. Independently, hz4x4 gudang re-run today at the same seed is **bit-identical in
float64** on every quantity (ATT 649.1999275624773, E 707.5668789808917, 2983 created, 2761 entered,
3600 observations) — a stronger statement than the corpus comparison can make, on a 16-intersection
scenario.

**Timing** (seconds per episode; the observed SUMO arms carry the halting cross-check on 1 of 5):

🚨 **A conflict P7.3 must not inherit.** Amendment F5 states hz4x4 at **438.7 s observed**, from the
pre-flight's PART 1 completion. **The campaign's own A4 chunk records 50.31 s and a re-measurement
today on an idle machine (load average 0.20) records 60.59 s, for the same code path, the same
scenario, the same seed and one episode** — and the two runs are bit-identical in their outputs, so
they did the same work. 438.69 s is an **8.7× outlier of undetermined cause**; the pre-flight ran
during an evening with three concurrent agent sessions and their sandboxes, which is the available
explanation but is not a measurement. **The rate P7.3 should schedule from is ~51–61 s per observed
hz4x4 episode** — consistent with the 51.0 s bare rate plus the ~19 % observer overhead measured on
hz1x1 — and the 438.7 s figure should not enter the freeze until it is reproduced.
