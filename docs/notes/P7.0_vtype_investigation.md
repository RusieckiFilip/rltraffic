# P7.0 prerequisite — vehicle-parameter parity between CityFlow and SUMO

**Date:** 2026-08-04 · **Run by:** Master chat, read-only investigation on `main` · **SUMO 1.27.1**
**Purpose:** establish, before the P7.0 brief is written, whether the recorded C3 landmine is real.

**Verdict: the recorded landmine is refuted, and a larger one is found in its place.**

---

## 1. What the plan currently says, and why it is wrong

Decisions Log 2026-07-31 records:

> **C3 LANDMINE — hangzhou vType is not bound in the shipped `.rou.xml`.** Its `<vehicle>` elements
> carry no `type` attribute, so SUMO runs them under `DEFAULT_VEHTYPE` (maxSpeed 55.55 m/s) while
> CityFlow uses the flow file's 11.11 m/s. A naive CityFlow→SUMO comparison on hangzhou would
> therefore measure a **5× speed-limit difference and attribute it to the simulator dynamics gap**.

The premise is right; the consequence is wrong.

**Right:** all four hangzhou variants define `<vType id="pkw" maxSpeed="11.111" .../>` and then bind it
to **0 of 2021** vehicles. Measured through traci, the vehicles genuinely run as `DEFAULT_VEHTYPE`.

**Wrong:** the 5× can never materialise, for two independently sufficient reasons.

1. **Lane speed limits cap it.** The hangzhou `.net.xml` files carry lane speeds of 11.11 m/s
   (24 lanes), 9.84, 9.26 and 3.65 — every one at or below CityFlow's 11.11. A vehicle's desired
   speed in SUMO is `min(vType.maxSpeed, laneSpeed × speedFactor)`, so a 55.55 m/s ceiling is never
   the binding constraint on this network.
2. **`speedFactor` is exactly 1.0**, measured, not the distribution some SUMO versions default to —
   so there is not even a tail of vehicles exceeding the lane limit.

remedy the plan prescribes — "bind vType" — does not fix the largest discrepancy.

## 3. A/B/C measurement — how much does each fix actually matter?

hangzhou bc-tyc, `sumo -n net -r rou --end 3600`, one run per variant, identical network and default
TLS program, so differences are attributable to the vehicle parameters alone.

| variant | arrived | mean TT (s) | mean speed | mean delay (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **a** as shipped (`DEFAULT_VEHTYPE`) | 1578 | 276.45 | 3.078 | 221.98 |
| **b** bind the existing `pkw` | 1564 | 278.17 | 3.045 | 221.76 |
| **c** CityFlow parity, i.e. **b + `tau=2.0`** | 1201 | 415.04 | 2.379 | 358.69 |

- **a → b: +0.6% travel time.** Binding the vType, the mandated C3 prerequisite, is a sub-1% effect.
- **b → c: +49% travel time, −23% arrivals.** The headway parameter dominates, by roughly two orders
  of magnitude more than the thing we were told to fix.

**Had P7.0 run as the plan prescribes, we would have bound the vType, declared the prerequisite
satisfied, and carried a ~50% travel-time confound into the headline C3 measurement.**

### Honest limits of this measurement
- **One scenario, one run per variant.** Not a population claim; hangzhou bc-tyc only.
- **`meanTT` here is over *arrived* vehicles only** (SUMO `tripinfo`), which is survivorship-biased —
  the exact trap `PREREGISTRATION.md` §3.1 pins down. Arrivals differ by 23% between a and c, and my
  own registered validity condition (§3.1: >5% population difference ⇒ comparison invalid) therefore
  applies to my own numbers. **The direction and the existence of a large effect are established; the
  magnitude is not trustworthy from these figures** and must be recomputed with `average_travel_time`
  over all vehicles that entered.
- **This is SUMO-vs-SUMO parameter sensitivity, not the CityFlow↔SUMO gap.** It answers "is the
  prerequisite real and sufficient?", not "how big is the dynamics gap?".

## 4. The picture across all six paired scenarios

| | CityFlow `flow.json` | SUMO as shipped | agree? |
|---|---|---|---|
| hangzhou ×4 | len 5.0, gap 2.5, acc 2.0, **hw 2.0**, vmax 11.11 | `DEFAULT_VEHTYPE`: len 5.0, gap 2.5, acc **2.6**, **tau 1.0** | len/gap yes; **accel and tau no** |
| cologne1 | len 4.3, gap 1.5, acc 2.6, **hw 1.5**, vmax 13.39 | `pkw`: len 4.30, gap 1.50, `speedFactor=normc(1.00,0.10,0.20,2.00)`; accel/tau inherit SUMO defaults (2.6 / **1.0**) | len/gap/accel yes; **tau no** |
| cologne3 | len 4.3, gap 1.5, acc 2.6, **hw 1.5**, vmax 13.39 | `pkw`: len 4.3, gap 1.5, `speedDev=0.1`; accel/tau inherit defaults | len/gap/accel yes; **tau no** |

Two consequences the plan does not currently record:

1. **Cologne's CityFlow parameters were evidently derived from its SUMO scenario** — length 4.3 and
   minGap 1.5 match to the digit, and CityFlow's `maxPosAcc` 2.6 equals SUMO's *default* accel. The
   one parameter that was not carried across is headway (1.5 vs SUMO's tau 1.0).
2. **The speed mismatch is a cologne problem, not a hangzhou one — the reverse of what is recorded.**
   Cologne lanes reach 27.78 m/s (plus one at 83.33, probably a special edge — not yet checked
   for whether traffic uses it), while CityFlow caps those vehicles at 13.39 m/s. So on cologne,
   SUMO vehicles *can* legally outrun their CityFlow counterparts, and cologne1 additionally draws
   `speedFactor` from a distribution reaching 2.0×. Hangzhou, where the plan puts the landmine, is the
   scenario where speed is already consistent.

## 5. What P7.0's brief must therefore require

- Parity is achieved by setting **`tau`** (and, on hangzhou, `accel`), not by binding a vType. Binding
  is necessary on hangzhou but nowhere near sufficient, and on cologne it is already done.
- **Full parity is impossible and must be declared rather than claimed.** CityFlow's `usualPosAcc` /
  `usualNegAcc` have no SUMO counterpart; SUMO's `sigma` (driver imperfection) and `speedFactor`
  distribution have no CityFlow counterpart. The paper states which parameters were matched and which
  have no analogue, or a reviewer finds the gap for us.
- The fix belongs in `offline/flow_randomizer.py::render_sumo`, which currently copies the template's
  `<vType>` verbatim and mirrors its binding — correct mirroring behaviour, and the right single place
  to introduce an explicit parity type. That file is ours, not frozen. No SUMO corpus exists yet, so
  changing it now costs nothing.
- Re-run this A/B with `average_travel_time` over all entered vehicles before quoting any magnitude.
