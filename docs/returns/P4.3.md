## 5. The withdrawn criterion, scored (`BRIEF_15` §14.1)

| set | Pearson `r`(in-support, ATT) |
|---|---|
| the six graded points `0 … −5762` | **+0.9801** (coordinator computed +0.9802 independently) |
| all nine declared grid points | **+0.5425** |

ATT is lower-is-better, so **positive means the criterion ranks the targets backwards.** Over the
graded upper region it does so almost perfectly.

**Why the two numbers differ, stated rather than smoothed:** the in-support fraction is
**non-monotone in the target** — it rises to exactly `1.0000` at `−9991` and then *falls* to 0.5415
at `−13000`, because the prompt now starts *below* the training floor. A single fraction conflates
two structurally opposite failures, which is why the artifact records `below` and `above` separately.
Over the full grid the correlation is diluted by that turn; over the region where the criterion is
monotone it is +0.98.

**Reported as a result, not an erratum:** a plausible, leakage-free criterion for return-prompt
selection turned out to be almost perfectly anti-correlated with the outcome it was meant to proxy.
That is the strongest evidence this task produces that conditioning a return-conditioned model
**outside** its training support is the mechanism operating as designed, not a defect to engineer
away — `dt_g0` is 0.0000 in-support and is the best point measured.

## 9. Deviations, disclosures and defects — including three of mine

### 9.1 Deviations from the brief and the approved plan

1. **Rule A's `k = 5` and `k = 20` ATT cells were dropped** (1,000 rollouts), by the same argument
   §12.6 uses against the secondary quantiles: three maxima of nested draw sets are neighbouring
   points in a space the grid already spans. The `k`-ablation is reported on the **targets** and on
   **`ratio_S(k)`** instead, where it is free and where it transfers to P7. Declared in the plan
   before the campaign; **18 minutes to reverse**.
2. **`p4_5_selection.json` → `p4_5_baselines.json`** in the regeneration proof (§8).

### 9.2 Test changes, disclosed in full

1. **T1b's tolerance replaced by an exact assertion.** It failed at `abs=1e-9`. Diagnosed before
   touching anything: `DTAgent._window` stores the quotient as **float32**, and this checkpoint's
   `rtg_scale` is 9991.0 — not a power of two — so the model receives the float32 round-trip, which
   differs from the float64 quotient by up to 1e−8 (**measured 5.814e−09**, and
   `float32(−4321/9991)` reproduces the obtained value **bit-for-bit**). The tolerance was the
   defect. It is now `==` against the float32 round-trip, which pins the storage convention as well
   as the arithmetic — **stricter, not looser** — and the override-before-load mutation was re-run
   against it and still kills it.
2. **Seven `match=` tokens tightened** after measuring their discriminating power with an AST pass
   (`DEFERRED` 43): `'probe'` matched **13** of the module's 46 raise messages, `'two routes'` 3,
   four others 2. Each was replaced by a strict substring extension of the **same** message.
   **Residual, disclosed:** `'checkpoint was saved at'` still matches two *character-identical*
   guards in `dt_gate.py:707` and `offline_baselines.py:2666`; only the first is reachable from
   `agent_with_target`, and making it unique would mean editing a merged module for a test's
   benefit.
3. **A fixture repaired after a mutation survived** — see §9.3.
4. **A test's expected value corrected** after the fixture gained jitter: it compared the artifact's
   correlation against the *nominal* inputs while the artifact correctly used the *realised* means.
   Now recomputed from the realised means read back out of the artifact.

### 9.3 Three defects of mine, found and fixed in this session

1. **I destroyed uncommitted work.** The mutation harness restores with `git checkout --`, which
   discards *every* uncommitted change, not only the mutation — it deleted an `evaluate_point`
   edit I had not yet committed. Re-applied and committed; the harness now **refuses to start on a
   dirty tree**. `BRIEF_15` §13.4 has since made the general form a `PROJECT_PLAN` §7 rule.
2. **A mutation survived because my fixture was degenerate.** M14 (`resolves` from the point
   estimate's sign) survived: the fixture's per-draw structure cancelled *exactly* under pairing, so
   every paired CI had zero width, every step "resolved", and the two rules were indistinguishable.
   Fixed by adding an independent per-(point, draw) jitter so the fixture contains **both** a step
   that resolves and one that does not, plus an assertion that the sign-based verdict **disagrees**
   with the CI-based one somewhere. M14 now dies.
3. **I committed a failing test.** My command was `pytest … | tail -4 && git commit …`; a
   pipeline's exit status is the last element's, so `tail`'s 0 masked pytest's 1 and the `&&` chain
   proceeded over a red suite. Caught on the next run, fixed, and disclosed in the repairing
   commit's message. **The failing commit is left in the branch history rather than rewritten.**
   `set -o pipefail` is now in use. Nothing was pushed and no reported number depended on it.

### 9.4 A near-miss worth recording

whose command line contained the string. Verified by reading `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` and the parent
chain, which showed the real process 3 minutes old and on schedule. *A process identified by a
