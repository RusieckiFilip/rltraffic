### 3.7 ⚠️ The finding I nearly reported as a bug: the DT clones fixed-time exactly

`dt@fixedtime` = 262.0706 and `behaviour@fixedtime` = 262.0706 — identical to four decimals, which is
the shape of a report that copied one arm into another's slot. **It is not.** Checked four ways:

- `dt@fixedtime` holds **n = 500** (5 seeds x 100 draws) against the behaviour arm's **n = 100**, so
  they are different objects;
- **all five independently-trained DT seeds produce identical per-draw ATT** — the five models are
  behaviourally indistinguishable;
- the DT's per-draw ATT equals the fixed-time policy's on **100 of 100 draws by exact float
  equality**;
- the two cell means differ **in the last bit** (`262.07062063447944` against `262.0706206344795`),
  which is precisely the residue of averaging 500 values against 100 — a copy would have been
  bit-identical.

**So on a deterministic, periodic behaviour policy the offline sequence model recovers the policy
exactly, and therefore cannot beat it.** The paired comparison reports this correctly but its test
statistics must not be quoted: **98 of the 100 per-draw differences are exactly 0.0** and the other
two are **−2.842e−14**, so Wilcoxon drops 98 zeros, runs on **n = 2**, and returns `p = 0.346`,
`r = −1.000` — **numbers about two float residues, not about policies.** The identity is the result;
the p-value is an artifact of it.

### 9.6a ⚠️ The `until`-loop self-match: I hit it, live, in the task whose brief warns about it

Before `BRIEF_17` §12 arrived, this session polled its own background jobs with

**No number in this packet came from a polled job**: every artifact was read from disk afterwards.
The pattern is retired from this task; §12's `tmux` ruling replaces it, and the campaign script
neither polls nor is polled.
