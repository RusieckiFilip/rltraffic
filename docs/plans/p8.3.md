## 1. §4 of the brief, verbatim — the structural differences, registered before the number exists

Copied without alteration from `docs/briefs/BRIEF_24_iql_d4rl_calibration.md` §4. These are the
candidate explanations if our IQL calibrates cleanly and the domain is what differs; they are recorded
now so they cannot be selected afterwards to fit the result.

> 1. **Single intersection.** OMIGA and OMSD are multi-agent SMAC, where *multimodal* means heterogeneous
>    **joint** policies across agents. Ours is **one agent under two policies**. Same word, different
>    quantity.
> 2. **Horizon.** D4RL locomotion is ~1000 dense-reward steps; we have **360 decisions**. Kumar et al.
>    show the offline-RL advantage **grows with H**, so a shorter horizon predicts less stitching benefit.
> 3. **Tuning.** Ours is untuned; the D4RL tables are post-tuning. **This cuts against us and must be
>    stated in that direction.**
> 4. **Mode geometry.** Our modes are **extremely disjoint — a 28,378-unit return gap with zero overlap**
>    (`DEFERRED` 46) — while medium-expert's are adjacent. **They may not be the same situation despite
>    sharing the label "bimodal".**

## 1b. Five further differences, on the implementation side, registered in the same breath

§4's four are about the *domain*. These five are about the *code*, they were found by reading
`offline/offline_baselines.py` and `agent/OfflineBaselines.py` before any run, and they are the
candidate explanations if our IQL calibrates **badly**. Registering them now is what stops the
post-hoc version of this list.

5. ⭐ **SINGLE Q, NO CLIPPED DOUBLE Q — the most consequential thing this plan registers.**
   `train_iql` builds one `q` and one `q_target` (`offline/offline_baselines.py:1960-1963`); published
   IQL uses **two** Q functions and takes their minimum. Ours has no such pessimism term. **This is a
   real deviation from published IQL and it is present in every merged P4.4–P4.7 number.**
   **It is registered, not repaired.** Changing it now would invalidate P4.4–P4.7 and would be a
   method re-specification made after seeing results — the worst available order of operations.

   🚨 **The consequence for how this task can be read, stated before the run:** a low score has
   **TWO** pre-declared explanations — the discretisation adapter (§3) and the single-Q design — and
   **the three-arm design controls only the first.** `bc` and `bc_top10` share the adapter, so a
   shared collapse localises the ceiling; **they do not share a Q function at all**, so nothing in
   this task can separate "single-Q hurts" from "our IQL is otherwise wrong". Reading rule **R-B**
   (§5.1) is written to stop at "the defect is IQL-side" and go no further, and §9 repeats the fence.
6. **Optimiser and schedule are the DT's, not IQL's.** `LEARNING_RATE = 1e-4`, `WEIGHT_DECAY = 1e-4`,
   `GRAD_CLIP = 0.25`, linear warmup over 1000 steps (`offline/dt_gate.py:126-134`), against published
   IQL's 3e-4 with a cosine schedule. Deliberate in P4.4 — the arms share a budget — and a difference
   here nonetheless.
7. **Architecture.** `MLPTrunk`: `d_model = 128`, three pre-LayerNorm residual feed-forward blocks,
   GELU, `dropout = 0.1` (`agent/OfflineBaselines.py:91-200`), against published IQL's 2x256 ReLU MLP.
   Dropout inside a value network is unusual and is ours.
8. **Discrete categorical policy.** AWR regresses a categorical log-probability onto the logged action
   (`:2022-2023`); published locomotion IQL uses a Gaussian policy and a Q that takes the action as an
   input. **This is the difference the adapter in §3 exists to bridge, and it cannot be bridged
   without cost.**
9. **Gradient budget.** Our declared budget is 40,000 steps at batch 1,280 (`DECLARED_GRADIENT_STEPS`,
   `IQL_BATCH_TRANSITIONS`) = 51.2 M sample visits; published IQL trains 1 M steps at batch 256 =
   256 M. §6.3 pre-registers what happens if this binds.

**What transfers exactly, and is worth stating in the other direction:** `tau = 0.7`, `beta = 3.0`,
`gamma = 0.99`, Polyak `0.005`, weight clip `100` and the `1000/(max_return - min_return)` reward scale
are the published locomotion values, so on this benchmark they are for once *in* their home domain.
And **halfcheetah never terminates early** — every D4RL halfcheetah episode ends by timeout — so the
project's no-`done` convention (contract C6, `iql_targets`) is exactly correct here rather than merely
tolerable. §5's Gate B asserts that property of the data instead of assuming it.

### 2.1 Three arms, not one — and why the third is not scope creep

The brief's Definition of Done names IQL alone. **A discretisation adapter is unavoidable (§3), and it
imposes an unknown ceiling on the achievable score, so an IQL number on its own cannot distinguish
"our IQL is broken" from "the adapter costs 30 points".** Running `bc` and `bc_top10` through the same
adapter, the same budget and the same rollout protocol controls that ceiling: the adapter is shared, so
the *ordering* survives it even when the levels do not.

It is also the direct measurement of the anomaly. The published ordering on this dataset is
**10%BC 92.9 > BC 55.2** with **IQL 86.7** between them; P4.7 found `bc_top10` leading IQL. Reproducing
or failing to reproduce that ordering with our own code is the sharpest available evidence about the
instrument.

**This is an addition to the brief's Definition of Done, not a substitution.** IQL's absolute number
against the published value is still the headline, and it is reported whether or not the other two
arms are run. Marked for the gate: *drop the BC arms if the coordinator wants the narrow version.*

## 3. The adapter, choice by choice

Our `train_iql` is discrete-action: the policy emits categorical logits over `n_actions` and Q emits
one value per action, gathered by an integer index. HalfCheetah's action space is `Box(-1, 1, (6,))`.
**The adapter is a codebook on the action space and nothing else. No line of `train_iql`, `train_bc`,
`iql_targets`, `MLPTrunk` or `masked_action_logits` is modified, and none is copied.**

| # | choice | what was chosen | why, and what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | how the continuous action becomes discrete | **k-means codebook, K = 64, fitted on the training actions** | The alternative — writing a continuous-action IQL (Gaussian policy, Q(s,a)) — would calibrate *new* code, not the code the paper's numbers flow through, which is the one thing the brief asks about. A codebook leaves the update byte-identical and moves the entire adaptation into the data. **It costs an unknown ceiling; §5 Gate C measures that ceiling directly rather than arguing about it.** |
| A2 | K = 64 | fixed **before** any run | 2^6 = 64 is exactly the number of corners of the action cube, the bang-bang controls; halfcheetah expert actions saturate heavily, and bang-bang policies are known to be competitive on this task (Seyde et al., *Is Bang-Bang Control All You Need?*, 2021). K = 8 (our own action-space width) and K = 256 are measured by Gate C's ceiling probe, which requires no training and therefore cannot be used to select a favourable K after the fact. |
| A3 | k-means implementation | numpy-only Lloyd's algorithm, k-means++ init, `np.random.default_rng(0)`, 25 iterations, fitted on a fixed 200,000-action subsample | No new dependency. Deterministic and re-runnable; the resulting codebook's sha256 goes in the artifact. Fitting on a subsample keeps it cheap; the subsample is drawn with a fixed seed before anything else happens. |
| A4 | encoding | nearest centroid in Euclidean distance, computed in float64, ties broken by lowest index | The obvious choice; stated because "nearest" needs a metric and a tie rule. |
| A5 | decoding at evaluation | the centroid itself, `clip(-1, 1)`, float32 | Centroids of in-range actions are in range; the clip is a guard, and the artifact records how often it binds (expected: never). |
| A6 | states | `(x - mean) / std` over the training observations, `std = 0 -> 1` | `NormalizationStats.normalize_state`, the repo's own convention, reused rather than reimplemented. It is also what published IQL does for locomotion. |
| A7 | rewards | `r * 1000 / (max_episode_return - min_episode_return)` | `iql_reward_scale`, unchanged, on episode returns. This is the published locomotion normalisation in its home domain. |
| A8 | transitions | `next_state` from the dataset's `next_observations` when present, otherwise the next row within the same episode; the last transition of each episode keeps its bootstrap | Matches C6: `build_transitions` reads observation row `T` for the final transition, and `iql_targets` carries no `done`. Gate B proves the dataset has no terminals, which is what makes this correct rather than convenient. |
| A9 | `bc_top10` selection | the top 10% of **episodes** by undiscounted return, whole episodes only | `TOP_RETURN_FRACTION` reused; whole-episode selection matches `top_return_streams`' unit (a stream, never a fragment). |
| A10 | BC input shape | windows of 20 consecutive positions, sampled within an episode | Keeps `train_bc`'s `(B, K, D)` contract and the 1,280-positions-per-step budget identical to IQL's. Feeding flat rows at batch 64 would have handed BC a 20x smaller budget. |
| A11 | what `avail_mask` is | all-`True`, width K | Every mask in our corpus is all-`True` (P4.4 docstring, P3 review), so this preserves the property the training code was written against; the mask still flows through `masked_action_logits` on the same code path. |

**What the adapter does not do:** it does not change any hyperparameter, does not add a second Q, does
not alter the loss, the optimiser, the schedule, the plateau diagnostic or the checkpoint format, and
does not touch a single file under `offline/` or `agent/`.

## 4. Where the code goes, and why it is not a package

```
calibration/                       NEW. Deliberately has NO __init__.py.
  README.md                        why the directory exists and how to run it
  d4rl_adapter.py                  numpy + torch only: codebook, normalised score,
                                   TransitionTable/stats construction, episode splitting
  d4rl_run.py                      CLI: reads the HDF5, trains, rolls out, writes the artifact.
                                   The only file that imports h5py / gymnasium / mujoco.
tests/test_d4rl_calibration.py     NEW. Adapter maths + the isolation guard.
docs/data/p8_3_d4rl_calibration.json   NEW. The artifact.
docs/plans/p8.3.md                 this file
docs/returns/P8.3.md               the Return Packet
```

**`calibration/` carries no `__init__.py` on purpose.** `tests/test_packaging.py`
(`top_level_package_dirs`, `test_every_top_level_package_is_packaged_or_explicitly_excluded`) requires
every top-level directory holding an `__init__.py` to appear in
`[tool.setuptools.packages.find].include`. `pyproject.toml` is `BRIEF_23`'s file and binding condition
(iii) forbids touching it, and widening `NOT_DISTRIBUTED` would mean editing two existing tests. **The
correct answer is also the compliant one: this code must not ship in the wheel**, because it depends on
packages outside the project's dependency set and no paper number flows through it. A directory that
is not a distributable package is exactly what that means. A new test asserts the absent `__init__.py`
with that reason, so the decision is pinned rather than assumed.

The split between `d4rl_adapter.py` and `d4rl_run.py` is what lets the project's own suite test the
load-bearing arithmetic: the adapter imports nothing the project venv lacks, so its tests run in the
normal `pytest` run, on every machine, forever. The runner is exercised by the calibration venv.

## 5. Pre-registered gates, thresholds fixed before the run

| gate | what it asserts | threshold | what a failure means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — provenance** | the downloaded HDF5's sha256, size, key set and row count are recorded; observations are 17-wide, actions 6-wide, in `[-1, 1]` | exact | wrong file; stop |
| **B — no terminals** | `terminals.sum() == 0` over the whole dataset | exact `== 0` | halfcheetah is the task whose timeouts make our no-`done` value learner correct. If terminals exist, the run is mis-specified and is reported as such, not adjusted |
| **C — quantisation ceiling** | open-loop replay from exact dataset states of (i) the recorded actions and (ii) their codebook decodings, on 20 held-out episodes; **all three of K = 8, K = 64 and K = 256 are fitted, replayed and reported** | reported, not gated | this is the number that tells a low score from a low ceiling. **Run and committed BEFORE any training step**, so K cannot be chosen on a training result. **RULED: K = 64 is approved because of this protocol, not because of the value** |
| **D — dynamics fidelity** | one-step reward reproduction: set `qpos = [0] + obs[:8]`, `qvel = obs[8:]`, apply the recorded action, compare the returned reward against the dataset's, over 10,000 sampled transitions | median abs difference < 1e-5, reported in full either way | `HalfCheetah-v4` under mujoco 3.11 is a substitute for the `mujoco-py` 2.1 environment that generated the -v2 data. If rewards do not reproduce, our evaluation environment is not the data-generating environment and **no normalised score from it is comparable to the published table** — that is a reported outcome, not a number to publish |
| **E — determinism** | the same seed re-run reproduces `canonical_state_dict_digest` exactly, for one IQL seed | byte-identical | the repo's standing determinism requirement |
| **F — isolation** | no `.py` outside `calibration/` and `tests/` imports `d4rl`, `mujoco`, `mujoco_py`, `gym` or `h5py` | zero hits | binding condition (ii) |

**Gate F's scope is wider than the brief's `offline/**` and its list excludes `gymnasium`**, which is a

### 5.1 Reading rules, fixed before the number exists

Let `S_iql` be our IQL's mean normalised score and `C = 86.7` the verified published value.

* **R-A.** `S_iql` within 10 points of `C`, **and** the ordering `bc_top10 > bc` reproduced →
  the implementation calibrates. §1b's R5 survives its first external check, and the P4.7 and grid4x4
  anomalies are licensed to be read as domain-side. **Not** as agreement with the literature about the
  domain (brief §5).
* **R-B.** `S_iql` far below `C` while `bc` and `bc_top10` land near their published 55.2 / 92.9 →
  the defect is IQL-side. The adapter is shared, so it cannot be the explanation. This is the outcome
  that must reach P5.2 before it inherits IQL as a baseline. **R-B stops there.** It may not name
  *which* IQL-side cause: §1b.5's single Q and §1b.6-9's optimiser, architecture and budget are all
  live, none is controlled by this design, and separating them needs variants this task is forbidden
  to build.
* **R-C.** All three arms far below their published values → the adapter's ceiling is binding and the
  measurement is **inconclusive about IQL**. Report it that way. Gate C's ratio is the evidence.
* **R-D.** Gate D fails → report the gate, publish no normalised score.

**No tuning under any branch.** The one pre-declared secondary is §6.3.

## 8. Tests, written before the implementation

All run in the **project** venv (they import only numpy/torch), except where marked.

1. `test_normalized_score_matches_the_d4rl_definition` — `100*(r - min)/(max - min)`; anchored at
   `score(REF_MIN) == 0.0` and `score(REF_MAX) == 100.0` exactly, and recomputed by an independent
   route on a random vector.
2. `test_the_reference_constants_are_the_published_ones` — pins `-280.178953` / `12135.0` with their
   provenance, so a silent edit is a failing test.
3. `test_codebook_encode_decode_round_trips_on_its_own_centroids` — encoding a centroid returns its
   own index; exact.

## 9. What this task may not conclude (brief §5, binding)

* It may **not** settle the mode-separability direction. That needs the TQ/SACo statistics across the
  eleven tiers and a modality statistic on both corpora; they are not in this brief.
* It may **not** be written up as agreement with the literature if it lands near the published value.
  It would show our *implementation* matches — a statement about the code.
* It answers exactly one question: **is our IQL implementation sound.**
* And one more, added here: **it cannot rule out §1b.5's missing clipped double Q as a cause of a low
  score**, because that would require running a variant we are forbidden to build. If R-B fires, the
  single-Q design is the first hypothesis, and naming it now is what makes that honest.
