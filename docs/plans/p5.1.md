## 1. Why this task exists

Every result the project holds comes from a setting where the Decision Transformer has **no
structural advantage.** BC, %BC and IQL are independent per intersection *by construction*, and so
is our DT today. `PROJECT_PLAN` §1b **R7** records the DT leading **zero of eight** tiers.

§1b's **R5** records that the deficit is robust to the **prompt** (P4.3: 0.9026 ATT across a
13,000-wide declared grid), to **data quality** (P4.6: five tiers) and to **the presence or absence
of a filter** (P4.7: three mixtures). Three independent knobs, none of which rescues it.

**Two explanations survive: the single-intersection setting, and the architecture itself.** This is
the only experiment that separates them, because it is the first time the DT is given information
the baselines structurally cannot use.

`PROJECT_PLAN` §9 rates collapse on 16-intersection grids on **prior evidence, not speculation** —
DTLight's own Table 1 reports its pure-offline variant at **446.8 ± 128.0** on Grid 4×4 from EMP
data, far worse than the behaviour policy it learned from. Section 4's **P3** is the criterion that
makes a collapse a *result* rather than a disappointment.

### 2.1 Adjacency — derived, and proved from structure alone

`PROJECT_PLAN` §6 describes P5.1 as graph attention "**from `RoadnetInfo`**". **That premise is
false.** `utils/common_utils.py` (63 lines, read in full) exposes no adjacency, neighbour or graph
field on either `RoadnetInfo` or `IntersectionInfo`, and `utils/` is frozen. Adjacency is therefore
**derived in `offline/`**, and proved before it is used:

> **`A` feeds `B` iff `A.outgoing_lanes ∩ B.incoming_lanes ≠ ∅`.**

| derivation, over the **16 controllable** intersections | directed | undirected | degree histogram |
|---|---|---|---|
| lanes, via `roadLinks` (the rule above) | 48 | **24** | `{2:4, 3:8, 4:4}` |
| **independent route**: roads' `startIntersection`/`endIntersection`, never touching lanes | 48 | **24** | — |
| **exact set equality of the two edge sets** | **True** | | |

That matches `BRIEF_22` §2's free positive control exactly: 4 corners at degree 2, 8 edges at 3,
4 interior at 4, **24 undirected edges.** The second route is a genuinely different object — road
endpoints rather than lane sets — and is the double-compute this project requires, not the same
algorithm retyped.

**🚨 THE TRAP, hit by the coordinator on the first attempt and reproduced here.**
`grid4x4_roadnet_red.json` holds **32** intersections and **`virtual` is `False` on all 32**; the
discriminator is **`gt_virtual`**, true on 16. Deriving over "non-virtual" therefore takes all 32:

| node set | directed | undirected | degree histogram |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TRAP** — all 32 "non-virtual" | 80 | **40** | `{1:16, 4:16}` |
| **CORRECT** — the 16 controllable | 48 | **24** | `{2:4, 3:8, 4:4}` |

⚠️ **The trap does not produce an obviously broken graph — it produces a *uniform* one**, in which
every real intersection reads degree 4 and the corners are indistinguishable from the interior. That
is precisely the plausible-but-wrong adjacency `BRIEF_22` §2 warns a mixing layer will happily train
on. *(The 16 extra nodes come out at degree **1**, not degree 0.)*

**Binding: the derivation is keyed off the corpus's own `ix_ids` / `env.intersections`, and asserts
`len == 16` and set equality with the roadnet's controllable set before deriving anything.** This is
`PROJECT_PLAN` §7's rule of 2026-08-16 — *any cross-system comparison must prove its pairing key
before using it, from structure alone* — applied to the pairing between corpus rows and graph nodes.

### 2.2 The corpus

`cf_grid4x4__mappo1000`, 5 seed directories x 40 episodes = **200 episodes**, draws 1–200 disjoint
across seeds. Per episode: `ix_ids = A0…D3`, **state_dim 40**, **n_actions 8**, homogeneous across
all 16; 361 observation rows, 360 decisions. `avail_mask` all-True, confirming `DEFERRED` 21 — **no
mask ablation is run and no paper sentence may credit masked-action modelling.**

State content (`states/base.py:213-270`): 12 incoming-lane counts + 12 incoming-lane waiting counts
+ 16-phase one-hot. **Only incoming lanes.** A node already sees the queues on its approaches — which
come from its neighbours — but never a neighbour's own state or phase. **That is the bound on what
spatial mixing can add, and it is recorded here so the result is not over-read in either direction.**

### 2.3 Per-intersection heterogeneity — the fact that decides the RTG rule

Sample: **tier `cf_grid4x4__mappo1000`, all 5 seed directories, 200 episodes, 16 intersections.**

- Global max total return **−64.0**; global min **−836.0**; global `max|.|` **836.0**.
- Own-max ranges from **−64.0** (`C2`) to **−142.0** (`D1`).
- Own-mean ranges from **−118.6** (`B2`) to **−244.6** (`C1`) — a **2.06x** mean spread.
- **15 of 16 nodes have an own-best that never reaches the global maximum.**

Normalisation statistics differ for **all 120 intersection pairs** (`A0` vs `C1`, max mean difference
**1.2161**), which is what makes `DEFERRED` 37 live here.

### 3.1 Architecture

One block, repeated `n_layer` times:

```
h = h + temporal_attn(ln(h), causal_bias)      # (B*N, 3K, d) -- reuses DTAgent's _attention_bias
h = h + spatial_attn (ln(h), adjacency_bias)   # (B*3K, N, d) -- adjacency, with self-loops forced open
h = h + mlp(ln(h))
```

`masked_action_logits`, `action_loss`, `_attention_bias` and `PAD_ACTION` are **imported** from the
merged P4 code, never reimplemented. The token layout, the left padding, the `PAD_ACTION = -1`
tripwire and the "the state token at `3t+1` predicts `a_t`" convention are inherited unchanged from
`agent/DTAgent.py` and `offline/dataset.py`.

### 3.2 Evaluation protocol — inherited by import

`offline.dt_gate.evaluate_arm`, `_cell`, `_paired`, `mean_ci95`, `wilcoxon_signed_rank` and
`env_settings_from_manifest`. Registered held-out pool **draws 1000–1099**, one episode per draw,
5 seeds, **500 episodes per cell**. A second implementation of the protocol is exactly how two arms
stop being comparable.

## 4. REGISTERED PREDICTIONS

**Scoring, common to all three.** Primary metric `att_horizon` (A1). Paired unit = per-draw mean over
the 5 seeds across the 100 shared held-out draws (`dt_gate._per_draw_means`). Interval = the 95 % CI
of the mean paired difference (`mean_ci95`, `ddof=1`, `1.96 sigma/sqrt(n)`). Lower ATT is better.
Every outcome is one of **HELD / FAILED / NOT RESOLVED**, and `NOT RESOLVED` is always reported with
the CI width rather than folded into either neighbour.

---

### P1 — spatial mixing beats its own no-mixing control on the same tier

Let `d = ATT(dt_spatial) - ATT(dt_nomix)` per shared draw.

- **HELD** iff the 95 % CI of `mean(d)` lies **entirely below 0**.
- **FAILED** iff it lies **entirely above 0** — the control is genuinely better. This is reported as a
  decisive result in the other direction, never folded into "inconclusive".
- **NOT RESOLVED** otherwise.

*Registered expectation:* **HELD.** This is the task's whole hypothesis and it is the one prediction
here I expect to hold. If it does not, the architecture explanation of §1b's R5 survives its own test
and the paper says so.

### P2 — the DT with mixing reaches the offline-method field it lost on all eight tiers

Two conjuncts, **scored and reported separately** — P4.7's M1 showed a conjunction whose parts have
different robustness must never be reported as one verdict:

- **P2a (rank):** `dt_spatial` has the **lowest held-out mean `att_horizon` of the five method arms**
  (`dt_spatial`, `dt_nomix`, `bc`, `bc_top10`, `iql`).
- **P2b (resolution):** its paired advantage over the **best-scoring other arm** resolves — CI
  entirely below 0.

**P2 HELDs only if both hold.** On the eight prior tiers the DT ranked 3/4, 3/4, 4/4, 2/4, 2/4 and
2/4 x3 — **never 1**, so P2a is falsifiable in exactly the way the eight-tier record makes precise.

*Registered expectation:* **FAILED.** %BC and IQL have beaten the DT at every rung measured so far,
and nothing about spatial mixing addresses why. Registered anyway because it is the question §1b's
R7 makes the paper answer.

### P3 — the declared collapse criterion

Reference arm: **`random` behaviour, rolled on the same 100 held-out draws**, so the criterion is a
**shared-draw** comparison and not an A5-void cross-draw-set one.

- **COLLAPSED** (for either DT arm) iff its held-out mean `att_horizon` is **worse than the `random`
  behaviour arm's**, with the paired CI **excluding 0**.
- Reported for `dt_spatial` and `dt_nomix` **separately**.

Two figures recorded beside it, neither of which is the threshold:
- `random`'s collected ATT on **training draws 1–200** is **257.7326** (`docs/data/att_ladder_v11.json`).
  A5 makes that cross-draw-set and therefore **void as a comparison**; it is recorded as a magnitude
  expectation only, and the criterion is scored against the freshly rolled held-out arm.
- DTLight's **446.8 ± 128.0** is on **their** Grid 4×4, from **their** EMP corpus, under **their**
  metric. It is the prior evidence that made §9 rate this risk High. **It is an illustration and
  explicitly not our threshold.**

*Registered expectation:* **not collapsed, but the criterion is registered because §9 rates it High
on prior evidence and `PREREGISTRATION` §10 already registers what we publish if it fires.**

### What is NOT registered here

The five-tier interaction ("does spatial mixing help *more* on poor data") is **P5.2's breadth and
§10's cut 2.** One tier is enough for the structural question; only the interaction needs five, and
the interaction is the expendable half.

## 5. The campaign

`offline/campaigns/p5_1.sh`, modelled on `offline/campaigns/p4_7_phase2.sh`: `set -euo pipefail`, the
thread pin **exported inside the script**, resumable at cell granularity with every skip logged by
name, and a final assertion that completed cells equal cells **derived from the declaration, never
from the files being checked**. Launched by the **user** in `tmux`. **No `until`-poll anywhere** —
`pgrep -f` matches the polling shell's own command line, measured in P4.6, and that hang is live in
this repo's history twice.

Estimated wall clock, from §2.4's measured per-seed figures: **~13 h** for the two DT arms, **~45 min**
for the three baseline arms, **~2.5 h** evaluation, **~1 h** anchors — **~17 h**, one overnight run.
**It is an estimate; only the ms/step figures are measurements.**

**Prerequisite — the held-out demand.** Only **5 of 100** held-out draws are materialised for
grid4x4. Draws 1000–1099 are regenerated with `offline.materialise_draws --env-config
configs/sim/cityflow_grid4x4.json`, and — copying **P7.0's Gate D** — proved **byte-identical on
`flow.json`** against the 5 survivors in the main tree **before any of them is used**, with the check
**shown FAILING on a deliberately perturbed draw**. `cityflow.json` differs by design (it carries an
absolute `dir`), so the identity is asserted on the demand bytes and on nothing else.

## 8. What I correct if a prediction fails

- **P1 FAILED or NOT RESOLVED** -> `PROJECT_PLAN` §1b gains a row: spatial mixing over true network
  adjacency, at the same capacity, does not recover the DT on a 16-intersection grid. That closes the
  *"single-intersection setting"* half of R5's pair and leaves the architecture as the standing
  explanation — a stronger and more publishable statement than P1 holding.
- **P2 FAILED** -> §1b's R7 extends from eight tiers to nine, and the claim constraint in §1 gains
  the multi-intersection case explicitly.
- **P3 COLLAPSED** -> reported as the main finding on *when* offline sequence modelling stops working,
  exactly as `PREREGISTRATION` §10 already registers. **Predicting a failure in advance and then
  observing it is a result; discovering it afterwards and reframing is not.**
