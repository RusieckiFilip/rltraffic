### 2.3 The corpus per tier, and the size match

Measured today by counting `.npz` files:

| tier | episodes | joint decision instants | streams |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mappo1000` (5 seed dirs × 40) | 200 | 72,000 | 3,200 |
| `maxpressure` (1 dir) | 200 | 72,000 | 3,200 |
| `random` (1 dir) | **400** | 144,000 | 6,400 |
| `fixedtime` (1 dir, optional) | 200 | 72,000 | 3,200 |

`random` holds two engine seeds per draw on **all three scenarios** — not only grid4x4. Left
unmatched, the `random` tier would train on twice the data at the same 40,000 steps, i.e. **17.8
epochs against every other tier's 35.6**, and tier would be confounded with training-set size. That
is the confound `PROJECT_PLAN` §7 (2026-08-12) records as having already misled us once, where
*"plateaued"* tracked training-set size rather than convergence.

> **D10 (B4): the `random` tier is size-matched by `one_per_draw`, with a declared RNG, and the
> selection is recorded in the declaration artifact.**
> ⚠️ **One deliberate deviation in the UNIT, stated because B4 says "200 streams" and that is P4.6's
> unit, not this scenario's.** On `cf_hz1x1` a stream *is* an episode, so "200 streams" and "200
> episodes" are the same object. On grid4x4 a stream is an `(episode, intersection)` pair, and
> selecting 200 of those would leave 12.5 episodes' worth of nodes — no joint window could be built,
> because `build_joint_index` refuses any decision instant missing an intersection. **The selection
> is therefore one EPISODE per draw: 200 episodes / 3,200 streams, which is the size match P4.6's
> rule expresses and is byte-for-byte the same corpus size as every other tier.** Verified precedent,
> as B4 requires rather than assumes: `docs/data/p4_6_grid.json` carries `"subsample":
> "one_per_draw"` with 200 training streams on the `random` tier and `"none"` elsewhere.

## 4. REGISTERED PREDICTIONS

**Scoring, common to all.** Primary metric `att_horizon` (A1). Paired unit = per-draw mean over the 5
seeds across the 100 shared held-out draws (`dt_gate._per_draw_means`). Interval = the 95 % CI of the
mean paired difference (`mean_ci95`, `ddof=1`, `1.96 σ/√n`). Lower ATT is better. Every outcome is
one of **HELD / FAILED / NOT RESOLVED**, and `NOT RESOLVED` is always reported with the CI width
rather than folded into either neighbour. **All of it is scored mechanically by the report generator
and reported whichever way it comes out.**

### 4.0 The prediction rule, stated as a function of quantities already on disk

**Rule R′ (behaviour-normalised tier transfer).** For a method arm `a` and tier `t`:

```
pred(a, t) = ATT_P5.1(a, mappo1000) · T(t) · I(a, t)

T(t)    = ladder(grid4x4, t) / ladder(grid4x4, mappo1000)                       # grid4x4's own tier movement
I(a, t) = [ATT_P4.6(h(a), t) / beh_P4.6(t)] / [ATT_P4.6(h(a), mappo1000) / beh_P4.6(mappo1000)]
```

with `h`: `dt_spatial, dt_nomix → dt`; `bc → bc`; `bc_top10 → bc_top10`; `iql → iql`. Sources:
`ATT_P5.1` from `docs/data/p5_1_grid.json` `cells`; `ATT_P4.6` and `beh_P4.6` from
`docs/data/p4_6_grid.json` `cells` and `behaviour_cells` (held-out); `ladder` from
`docs/data/att_ladder_v11.json` (training draws).

For the new arm, which has no `mappo1000` cell to anchor on:
`pred(bc_top10_perix, t) = pred(bc, t) · [ATT_P4.6(bc_top10, t) / ATT_P4.6(bc, t)]`, and at
`mappo1000` `= ATT_P5.1(bc, mappo1000) · [ATT_P4.6(bc_top10, mappo1000) / ATT_P4.6(bc, mappo1000)]`.
**Reason it is the right referent:** on a one-intersection scenario the per-intersection filter *is*
the global filter, so `cf_hz1x1`'s %BC-over-BC ratio is the per-intersection filter's own measured
effect.

Anchors: `pred(behaviour, maxpressure) = ladder(grid4x4, maxpressure) = 167.4920`;
`pred(behaviour, random) =` the shared `random` cell `= 260.3602` (D12).

**A simpler rule was computed first and REJECTED before the run, and the rejection is recorded here
rather than left invisible.** The naive form `pred = ATT_P5.1(a, mappo1000) · [ATT_P4.6(a,t) /
ATT_P4.6(a, mappo1000)]` transfers `cf_hz1x1`'s **raw** tier ratio, which on that scenario is
dominated by its behaviour policy being 4.07× worse on `random` than on `mappo1000`. On grid4x4 the
same behaviour ratio is **1.61**. The naive rule therefore double-counts the demand difference and
predicts e.g. `dt_spatial@random` at 790.69 against R′'s 312.90. **One rule is registered, not two.**

**Calibration, stated beside the band as B5.3 requires.** The only out-of-sample test available for
R′'s core assumption — that a behaviour-normalised excess transfers — is to run it across
**scenarios**: predict P5.1's grid4x4 `mappo1000` cells from `cf_hz1x1`'s at the same tier
(`pred = ladder(grid4x4, mappo1000) · ATT_P4.6(a, mappo1000) / beh_P4.6(mappo1000)`):

| arm | predicted | measured | relative error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dt_spatial` | 159.3809 | 197.4126 | **+23.9 %** |
| `dt_nomix` | 159.3809 | 157.8477 | **−1.0 %** |
| `bc` | 159.6963 | 168.9806 | **+5.8 %** |
| `bc_top10` | 156.6581 | 749.5796 | **+378.5 %** |
| `iql` | 157.1344 | 275.8354 | **+75.5 %** |

**Median 23.9 %, max 378.5 %, and 3 of 5 inside ±30 %.** That is a weak rule and it is registered as
one. The tier transfer registered below is an easier problem than this calibration measured — it
holds the scenario fixed and anchors on grid4x4's own cells — which is exactly why the threshold in
Q1 is set **above** the calibrated rate rather than at it.

### Q0 — THE HEAD COUNT, AND THE STOP RULE (A2). Reported FIRST.

The head arm is the 2×2 `{dt_spatial, dt_nomix} × n_head ∈ {1, 4}` at `mappo1000`. The 1-head pair is
P5.1's, reused verbatim. Let, per shared draw,

```
d1 = ATT(dt_spatial@1H) − ATT(dt_nomix@1H)        measured: +39.5649, CI [+36.0510, +43.0787]
d4 = ATT(dt_spatial@4H) − ATT(dt_nomix@4H)        to be measured
 I = d4 − d1                                       the reported quantity
```

**`I` is the headline of phase A**, with both simple effects beside it. `d1` and `d4` are computed on
the same 100 draws and the same 5 seeds, so `I` is a paired per-draw quantity with its own CI.

- **Q0a — HELD** iff the CI of `mean(d4)` lies entirely **above** 0 (mixing still hurts at 4 heads).
  **FAILED** iff entirely below. **NOT RESOLVED** otherwise.
- **Q0b — HELD** iff the CI of `mean(I)` **includes** 0 (the head count does not change the
  contrast). **FAILED** otherwise, with its sign reported.

> 🚨 **THE STOP RULE, declared as §2 requires and enforced mechanically by the campaign script rather
> than remembered: if the 95 % CI of `mean(d4)` lies ENTIRELY BELOW 0 — spatial mixing helps at 4
> heads — the ladder sweep is NOT RUN. The campaign exits after phase A and the task reports the head
> result.** A CI that straddles zero is **not** a reversal and phase B proceeds; that case is
> reported as *"at 4 heads the contrast does not resolve"*.

*Registered expectation:* **Q0a HELD, Q0b HELD** — the harm persists and the head count does not
change it. ⚠️ Stated against my own interest: if Q0a fails, P5.1's headline result is
configuration-specific and this task says so as its first sentence.

⚠️ **What Q0 may NOT be called:** a GAT-head ablation. D13 — `n_head=4` raises the **temporal** head
count too, in both arms.

## 5. The campaign

`offline/campaigns/p5_2.sh`, modelled on `offline/campaigns/p5_1.sh`: `set -euo pipefail`, the thread
pin **exported inside the script**, resumable at seed granularity for training and cell granularity
for evaluation, every skip logged **by name**, and a final assertion that the completed cells equal
the cells **derived from the declaration, never from the files being checked**. Launched by the
**user** in `tmux`. **No `until`-poll anywhere** — `pgrep -f` matches the polling shell's own command
line and that hang is live in this repo's history twice. `mkdir -p` of the log directory happens
**before** `tee` opens its target, in the launch instructions and not only in the script, and the
script never clears the log directory afterwards (P4.7 and P5.1 each lost a log this way).

**Phase order, and it is the stop rule's mechanism:**

```
GATE 0  re-materialise held-out draws 1000-1099; prove byte-identity on flow.json against the
        5 survivors in the main tree, shown FAILING on a perturbed draw
GATE 1  re-verify output/SHA256SUMS_p5_1.txt AT CONSUMPTION; re-roll the `random` anchor and
        require EXACT equality with P5.1's 500 episodes.  MISMATCH => REFUSE AND STOP (B3c)
PHASE A dt_spatial_h4, dt_nomix_h4 @ mappo1000; evaluate; score Q0
        >>> if CI(d4) lies entirely below 0: write STOPPED_BY_RULE and EXIT.  No phase B. <<<
PHASE C bc_top10_perix @ mappo1000 (train + evaluate)
PHASE B maxpressure, then random: 6 method arms + the tier's behaviour anchor
REPORT  assemble, score every prediction, emit every ordering with its per-seed count and range
```

Only **5 of 100** held-out draws are materialised for grid4x4 in the main tree; P5.1's worktree,
which held the other 95, no longer exists (`git worktree list`, run today). Gate 0 regenerates them
with `offline.materialise_draws --env-config configs/sim/cityflow_grid4x4.json` and proves them
against the survivors exactly as P5.1 did.

**Rehearsal knobs** (`P52_STEPS`, `P52_WORK`, `P52_OUT`) exist so the control flow — the resume
branches, the stop rule and the completeness assertion — is **executed** before handover rather than
read. The completeness assertion reads the declared budget out of every cell and refuses anything but
40,000, so a rehearsal can never masquerade as a campaign.

**CUDA is mandatory** and the script refuses to start without a visible GPU: P5.1 measured the same
model at 5440 ms/step on CPU, which is 60 h per seed.

## 8. What I correct if a prediction fails

- **Q0a FAILED** (mixing helps at 4 heads) → the ladder is not run; `PROJECT_PLAN` §1b gains a row
  saying P5.1's negative result is specific to the single-head configuration, and §1's claim
  constraint on architecture hyperparameters is the sentence that changes. **This is the outcome that
  most damages the work already merged, which is why the rule is mechanical rather than mine to
  apply.**
- **Q0b FAILED** → the head count interacts with spatial mixing, and `DEFERRED` 52's full sweep stops
  being optional.
- **Q1 / Q2 FAILED** → R′ is reported as a rule that does not transfer across tiers, with the k-of-N
  and the concordance counts. That is a finding about the transferability of `cf_hz1x1`'s grid, and
  it bears directly on how much of P4.6 may be generalised.
- **Q3 FAILED** (the DT's lead does not survive) → §1b's R7 extends from eight tiers to eleven and
  A1's ninth-tier observation is reported as **tier-specific**, which is the honest reading and the
  weaker one.
- **Q3 HELD** → the paper gains a measured, out-of-sample, pre-registered statement that an
  independent-per-intersection DT leads the offline field on a 16-intersection network across the
  ladder. ⚠️ **It still may not be written as "the DT leads" without the `dt_nomix` fence** (§1) **and
  without §1b R7's descriptive-count constraint** — no sentence derived from a count of orderings
  carries inferential weight, because no family-wise correction exists and P8.1 has not run.
- **Q4b FAILED** → the interaction §10's cut 2 called the expendable half is measured and absent; the
  harm is reported as a level effect that does not depend on data quality.
- **Q5 FAILED** → the load-sorter mechanism is `mappo1000`-specific, which would contradict §2.4's
  corpus measurement and would be the most surprising outcome in this file.
- **Q6 fires** → reported as the main finding on *when* offline sequence modelling stops working,
  exactly as `PREREGISTRATION` §10 already registers. **Predicting a failure in advance and then
  observing it is a result; discovering it afterwards and reframing is not.**
